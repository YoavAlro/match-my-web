Field notes · North channel

The last wooden ferry still knows every current

Captain Mara Venn starts before the streetlights blink off. She checks the tide against a pencil mark inside the wheelhouse, then listens for the low knock of water beneath the cedar hull.

The ferry carries students, night-shift nurses and three bicycles on its first run. “A timetable gets you across,” Mara says. “Knowing the water gets everyone home.”

Food · Lantern Street

The bakery that starts its day at midnight

At Little Gull, midnight is morning. Flour lifts through the lamplight while baker Sela Orr shapes sesame loaves for the docks, the school kitchen and whoever follows the smell through the blue door.

Nothing waits under glass. By eight, the racks are bare and the bakers sit down together for the first quiet cup of tea.

City · Mariner House

A rooftop garden learns to drink the rain

Twenty-four planters now hold enough rain to soften the rush through Mariner House’s old pipes. The same water feeds tomatoes, fennel and a wall of climbing beans.

Residents keep the watering rota beside the lift. Most visits take five minutes; few conversations stay that short.

Culture · 91.3 FM

At 91.3, the whole neighborhood gets a voice

The studio is one room above a repair shop, insulated with old theater curtains. Its signal barely clears the ridge, but every evening the request line fills before the weather report ends.

Between songs, callers share lost gloves, late buses and poems written on break. The station’s size is the reason it can listen.

People · East tide pool

Dawn swimmers reclaim the old tide pool

The blue ladder goes in at 5:42. No one remembers choosing the time; it simply became the minute when towels appear along the wall and the first brave swimmer tests the water.

The club has no membership list. Bring a warm drink for someone else, they say, and you already belong.

Archive · Market Row

The sign painter keeping old storefronts legible

Niko Vale removes paint one careful layer at a time. Under a pharmacy’s plastic lettering, he finds a grocer’s gold leaf and the shadow of a name no directory remembers.

He photographs every layer before restoring the newest sign. The archive lives in flat boxes, arranged by street and the color first exposed.
